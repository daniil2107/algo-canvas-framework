# Worked example: portable rain shelter

This is a synthetic early-stage design exercise. It proposes concepts without establishing structural or weather performance.

## Brief

Provide temporary rain protection on paved ground. No ground penetration is allowed. Transport and deployment are manual. A waterproof flexible membrane or overlapping rigid panels may be considered. Dimensions, design wind load, acceptable mass, and drainage details remain unresolved.

## Chart

| Function | Option A | Option B | Option C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Support the weather barrier | F1A: folding rigid arches | F1B: inflatable tubes | F1C: existing external supports |
| Shed rain | F2A: flexible waterproof membrane | F2B: overlapping rigid panels | — |
| Stabilize against motion | F3A: ground stakes | F3B: portable ballast | F3C: clamps to existing structure |
| Compact for transport | F4A: fold into a flat bundle | F4B: roll around a 40 mm mandrel | F4C: nest disassembled segments |

The chart contains 3 × 2 × 3 × 3 = 54 theoretical combinations. This is a count of selections, not feasible concepts.

## Compatibility checks

| Combination / condition | Assessment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Any concept using F3A on the specified site | Conflicting | Stakes require ground penetration, which the brief prohibits. |
| F2B + F4B, assuming unsegmented rigid panels | Conflicting | Rigid panels cannot roll around the specified mandrel. Segmentation would change the selected principle and require a revised chart. |
| F1C or F3C | Unresolved | Suitable external structures and permission to use them have not been established. |
| F1B + F2B | Unresolved | Panel attachments and tube load capacity require analysis. Novelty alone does not justify rejection or acceptance. |

Packing is coupled to support and barrier choices. For example, a concept using folding arches and a membrane can fold as a bundle; a concept using segmented rigid arches and panels might nest after disassembly. These are architecture-dependent interfaces, not independent dropdown choices.

## Two candidate concepts

**C1: folding-arch shelter.** F1A + F2A + F3B + F4A. A membrane spans folding arches, with ballast attached through defined load paths. The membrane and arches fold together. Questions include ballast requirements, joint strength, ponding, and package size.

**C2: inflatable shelter.** F1B + F2A + F3B + F4B. Deflated tubes and the membrane roll for transport. Rigid pump and ballast parts travel separately. Clarify the package boundary before claiming the entire system rolls around the mandrel. Questions include inflation equipment, pressure retention, puncture tolerance, and wind stability.

The two concepts differ in load-bearing principle. Neither has demonstrated feasibility. A third concept using external structures remains conditional on site evidence.

## Next step

Define dimensions and environmental loads, then estimate stability and support capacity. Those results may revise both the chart and the permitted concept set.
